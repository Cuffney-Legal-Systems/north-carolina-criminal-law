# NC Defenses Catalog

Every defense in Part III of the N.C. Pattern Jury Instructions, plus the
non-instruction defenses that are really failures of the State's proof — mapped
to what triggers each one, who carries the burden, and what develops it.

**How to use this file.** Sweep the record against the **Triggers** column. Any
hit means: load the instruction from
`$JI_DIR/reference/instructions/<number>.md` and analyze it in Section 4 —
whether or not anyone in the record used the defense's name. A trigger is a
fact, not a label. The client found *"on the ground underneath"* the complainant
triggers self-defense even though the report never says the words.

**Two rules, both hard:**

1. **Take the burden from the loaded instruction, never from this table.** The
   burden column below is a routing aid. The instruction's own mandate paragraph
   is the authority, it is what the judge will read to the jury, and it is what
   goes in the analysis. Revision years move; this file does not.
2. **Never invent a defense.** A defense is analyzed only where the record
   supplies a fact that reaches it. Where nothing does, Section 4 says so in one
   sentence and names what was looked for. Constructing a version of events is
   outside what this skill does — RPC 3.3, 3.4, 1.2(d).

**The burden distinction matters and is frequently gotten backwards.** In North
Carolina some defenses put nothing on the defendant — once raised by the
evidence, the **State** must disprove them beyond a reasonable doubt. Others the
defendant must prove **to the jury's satisfaction** (a lower bar than beyond a
reasonable doubt, but still the defendant's to carry). Getting this wrong in a
memo produces a bad trial decision, so it is verified against the instruction
text every time.

## Justification and excuse

| Defense | Instruction | Triggers in the record | Burden (verify against instruction) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Self-defense — assaults involving deadly force** | *308.45* (rev. 2023) | Any deadly-force assault or shooting where the complainant was armed, advancing, an initiator, or physically engaged with the client; client's injuries; client found underneath or grappling; "he came at me" statements | **State** must prove beyond a reasonable doubt the client did **not** act in self-defense |
| **Self-defense — assaults not involving deadly force** | *308.40* (rev. 2023) | Simple assault, affray, mutual combat, a shove or strike after being touched or threatened first | **State** disproves |
| **Retreat** | *308.10* (rev. 2022) | Used **after** a self-defense instruction wherever retreat, stand-your-ground, or the client's lawful presence is in issue | Follows the self-defense instruction |
| **Defense of a family member or third person** | *308.47* (non-deadly), *308.50* (deadly), *308.60* (homicide) | The client intervened on behalf of another person who was being attacked or threatened | **State** disproves |
| **Defense of habitation, workplace, or motor vehicle** | *308.80* (rev. 2024) | Force used against someone unlawfully entering or attempting to enter a home, workplace, or vehicle; G.S. 14-51.2 presumption may apply | **State** disproves; note the statutory presumption |
| **Self-defense to sexual assault (homicide)** | *308.70* (rev. 2022) | Force used to prevent a sexual assault | **State** disproves |
| **Defensive force unavailable — felony exception** | *308.90* (rev. 2022) | **Read this one against the client.** The State will use it where the client was committing, attempting, or escaping from a felony. Analyze whether the exception actually applies before relying on self-defense | State's rebuttal instruction |
| **Detention of offenders by private persons** | *308.41* (rev. 2009) | The client detained someone they believed had committed an offense — G.S. 15A-404 | Per instruction |
| **Accident — homicide** | *307.10* (rev. 2003) | Unintentional killing; "it just went off"; a struggle over a weapon | **State** must disprove |
| **Accident — non-homicide** | *307.11* (rev. 2020) | Unintentional injury or damage; the same trigger facts outside homicide | **State** must disprove |
| **Necessity** | *310.12* (rev. 2022) | Conduct compelled by a natural or physical emergency with no reasonable legal alternative | Defendant, **to the jury's satisfaction** |
| **Justification** | *310.14* (rev. 2022) | Most often litigated on firearm-by-felon and similar possessory offenses where the client took possession under imminent threat | Defendant, **to the jury's satisfaction** |
| **Compulsion, duress, or coercion** | *310.10* (rev. 2022) | Third-party threat of immediate death or serious bodily harm; the client acted under another's direction under threat | Defendant, **to the jury's satisfaction** — not beyond a reasonable doubt |
| **Duress or necessity — escape from DOC** | *310.11* (rev. 2003) | Escape charges where conditions of confinement are asserted | Defendant, to satisfaction |

## Capacity and state of mind

| Defense | Instruction | Triggers in the record | Burden (verify against instruction) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Insanity** | *304.10* (rev. 2009) | Documented mental illness, commitment history, bizarre conduct at the scene, medication noncompliance noted by officers | Defendant, **to the jury's satisfaction** (not beyond a reasonable doubt). Note the verdict and commitment consequences before recommending it |
| **Automatism or unconsciousness** | *302.10* (rev. 2009) | Seizure, blackout, sleepwalking, concussion, hypoglycemia, involuntary intoxication | Per instruction — analyze separately from insanity |
| **Voluntary intoxication — in general** | *305.10* (rev. 2020) | Documented intoxication where the charge requires specific intent. **Not** a defense to general-intent offenses — say so rather than raising it | Per instruction |
| **Voluntary intoxication — premeditation and deliberation** | *305.11* (rev. 2009) | First-degree murder on a premeditation theory with intoxication evidence | Per instruction |

**Capacity is not competency.** Nothing in this catalog addresses capacity to
proceed under G.S. 15A-1001 *(verify)*. Where the record raises a question about
the client's present ability to assist counsel, that is a Section 7 action item
for counsel — not a Section 4 defense.

## Procedural and identity defenses

| Defense | Instruction | Triggers in the record | Burden (verify against instruction) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alibi** | *301.10* (rev. 2003) | Any documented account placing the client elsewhere; timestamps, receipts, phone records, a witness statement, employment records | **The burden of proving an alibi does not rest on the defendant** — a point frequently misremembered |
| **Entrapment** | *309.10* (rev. 2021) | Undercover or informant-initiated transactions; repeated solicitation; conduct originating with law enforcement | Defendant, **to the jury's satisfaction** |
| **Lack of jurisdiction** | *311.10* (rev. 2003) | Any element occurring outside North Carolina; a boundary or federal-enclave question | Per instruction — comes with a special verdict form |
| **Accepted medical purpose** | *306.10* (rev. 2020) | Penetration alleged in a medical or caregiving context | Per instruction |

## Failures of the State's proof (no defense instruction — these are element attacks)

These are not affirmative defenses and must never be blended with the ones
above. They are arguments that the State has not carried its own burden, and
**nothing shifts to the client**. They belong in the Charge Analysis attack
column and get a short Section 4 note.

| Argument | Where it lives | Supporting instruction | Triggers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mere presence** | Acting-in-concert / conspiracy block | *202.10*, *202.80* | The client's only documented link is proximity or association; the report says "the suspects" and "they" without attributing conduct |
| **Misidentification** | Section 5 | *104.90*; *104.98* where a photo array was used | Any contested identification — see the identification checklist |
| **Lack of intent** | Element table | The charge's own instruction | Specific-intent offenses where the record documents conduct but no fact bearing on purpose |
| **No possession** | Element table | *104.41* (actual-constructive possession) | Shared vehicles and residences; items not on the client's person; non-exclusive control |
| **Claim of right** | Element table | The larceny instruction's own language | A documented dispute over ownership or a belief the property was the client's |
| **Mistake of fact** | Element table | The charge's own instruction | A documented factual belief negating a required mental state |
| **Consent** | Element table | The charge's own instruction | Where consent negates an element (trespass, some assaults, some sexual offenses — check the offense's own treatment) |
| **Insufficient evidence of a status element** | Element table | The charge's own instruction | Prior conviction, prior order, or status element assumed but not documented in the production |
| **Third-party guilt** | Section 3 | — | Another person with motive, opportunity, or a matching description. Admissibility must be researched *(verify)* before it is relied on |
| **Variance** | Section 3 | — | The charging language does not match the conduct, victim, property, date, or subsection the report describes |

## Instructions worth requesting even where no defense applies

Not defenses, but defense leverage — the cautionary and evidentiary instructions
counsel can ask for. Name them in Section 3 or 5 next to the weakness that earns
them, and again in Section 7 if a written request is warranted.

| Instruction | What it does | Request it when |
|---|---|---|
| *101.10* | Burden of proof and reasonable doubt | Always |
| *101.30* | Defendant's decision not to testify | The client will not testify |
| *101.42* | Multiple defendants — guilt determined separately | Any joint trial |
| *104.05* | Circumstantial evidence | The case rests on inference |
| *104.20* | Testimony of an interested witness | A witness with a stake in the outcome |
| *104.25* | Accomplice testimony for the prosecution | A codefendant or cooperating witness testifies |
| *104.30* | Informer or undercover agent | An informant is involved |
| *104.35* | Flight | **Against the client** — anticipate it where the record documents flight, and consider what limits it |
| *104.40* | Doctrine of recent possession | **Against the client** — anticipate it in larceny and B&E cases; check the timing requirement |
| *104.41* | Actual-constructive possession | Any contested possession |
| *104.60 / 104.70* | Admissions / confessions | A statement is in evidence — and check which one the statement actually is |
| *104.90* | Identification of a defendant as perpetrator | Any contested identification |
| *104.98* | Photo lineup requirements | A photo array was used, especially where EIRA compliance is questionable |
| *105.20* | Impeachment or corroboration by prior statement | A witness's account changed |
| *105.21* | False, contradictory, or conflicting statements of the defendant | **Against the client** — anticipate it where the client gave more than one account |

Instructions marked **against the client** are the State's, not the defense's.
They are listed here because a defense memo that maps only the favorable
instructions has told counsel half of what happens at the charge conference.
