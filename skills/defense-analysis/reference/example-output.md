# Worked Example — Target Length and Format

**Fictional case, invented facts.** This exists to show the *shape and density* of
a finished defense analysis, not to supply law or facts.

It is deliberately the **same fictional case** as the worked example in the
sibling `prosecutors-analysis` skill — read side by side, the two show how the
identical record produces a different document depending on which side of the
courtroom the reader sits. The prosecution version screens for probable cause on
the alleged gunman. This one is written for counsel appointed to the **second
man**, whose entire exposure runs through acting in concert and conspiracy.

What to notice:

- **Section 1 is the largest fixed section.** It carries the whole narrative
  record — and every sentence is attributed to whose account it comes from. The
  investigative chronology records the steps that produced nothing and the items
  that were referenced but never produced.
- **Section 3 is the longest section overall.** It is organized under labeled
  sub-headings and every bullet ends in a **Use:**. That is the difference
  between a defense memo and a critique.
- **Element verdicts are honest.** Element 1 of the robbery is **STRONG** and
  gets no attack — because the value of the **NO PROOF** verdicts depends
  entirely on the credibility of the strong ones.
- **The acting-in-concert theory gets its own table.** In a multi-defendant case
  it is frequently the whole case, and burying it inside the substantive element
  table hides it.
- No section explains a legal concept. No fact is stated twice.

> **★ BEST ISSUE — no conduct attributed to the client:** the record describes the
> client standing at the door *"without speaking"*; the only act attributed to him
> is three words a witness heard once, in a dark lot, from a man she could not
> identify. *See Charge 1 acting-in-concert block and §3.*

> **★ CONFLICT / BRUTON — codefendant's 06/11 statement implicates the client:**
> Doe 1 told Det. Alvarez the client *"told him to do it."* Severance and
> counsel-conflict screening are immediate. *See §3 and §7.*

## 1. SUMMARY

### 1A. The State's account of the incident

The State's account comes almost entirely from one witness. Jane Doe 1 told Ofc.
Reyes that at approximately 11:40 p.m. on Tuesday, June 2, 2026, she was walking
alone from her apartment to her car in the surface lot in the 400 block of Sample
Street. She said the lot was dark — *"the lot lights were out"* — and that she
noticed two men crossing toward her from the sidewalk behind her (Narr. #1).

She stated the **taller** man came to within a few feet, displayed what she
described as a black handgun at waist level, and said *"give it here."* She
stated the **second, shorter man stood at her driver's door without speaking.**
She said she handed over her purse immediately; she was not touched and reported
no injury. She told Ofc. Reyes that before the taking she heard the shorter man
say *"run that"* to the taller one (Narr. #1).

Ofc. Reyes wrote that both men then ran east on foot toward Example Avenue. Jane
Doe 1 called 911 from the lot at 11:43 p.m. She described the gunman as a man in
his twenties wearing *"an orange sweater"* and the second man only as wearing
*"dark clothing"* — the report records no description of the second man's build,
race, age, height, or face (Narr. #1; 911 log). The purse contained her wallet,
keys, and a phone charger; no value is itemized anywhere in the report.

Ofc. Reyes's narrative characterizes both men as *"the suspects"* throughout and
does not, after the first paragraph, distinguish which man is described in any
given sentence.

### 1B. Investigative chronology

- **06/02/2026, 2343** — 911 call received; dispatched as armed robbery from
  person. Ofc. Reyes arrived 2347 and took the account above (Narr. #1).
- **06/02/2026, 2351** — Ofc. Reyes stopped Doe 1 and **the client** four blocks
  east on Example Avenue, eleven minutes after the call. **The purse was
  recovered from Doe 1's waistband.** An item logged as *"airsoft pistol, orange
  tip removed"* was recovered from **the client** (Narr. #1; property log).
  Narr. #1 states no basis for the stop beyond *"matched the description"* — the
  only description broadcast was *"orange sweater"* and *"dark clothing."*
- **06/02/2026, 2351** — **BWC: no recording from the stop appears in the
  production.** The BWC log lists Ofc. Reyes as equipped. **NOT IN PRODUCTION.**
- **06/03/2026, 0012** — **Street show-up** at the stop location. Jane Doe 1
  identified Doe 1 as the gunman. **As to the client she said only *"I think
  that's the other one."*** **Doe 1 was booked in a red hoodie**; no orange
  sweater was recovered from either man or along the route (Narr. #1).
- **06/03/2026** — Doe 1 interviewed at intake. He stated only *"I found the
  purse."* He did not name the client (Det. Alvarez supplement 06/03).
- **06/03/2026** — Supplement records that **the client declined to speak**. No
  statement was taken; no Miranda documentation appears in the production.
- **06/04/2026** — **Photo array** shown to John Doe 3, a bystander who called in
  a second 911 report. He *"was unable to identify any of the subjects."* The
  array, the administrator's instructions, and the filler photographs are **NOT
  IN PRODUCTION** (Det. Alvarez supplement 06/04).
- **06/04/2026** — Supplement notes the 400-block businesses *"were not
  canvassed"* for surveillance. **No canvass has been recorded since**, and no
  footage is in the file.
- **06/09/2026** — Property log records the airsoft pistol into evidence. **No
  firearms-unit examination has been requested or reported.** The purse was
  never processed for latent prints; no request appears in the file.
- **06/11/2026** — Second interview of Doe 1. He stated the client *"told him to
  do it."* Doe 1 was charged the same day (Det. Alvarez supplement 06/11).

## 2. CHARGE ANALYSIS

### Charge 1 — Robbery with a dangerous weapon, G.S. 14-87 (Class D)
*Elements per N.C.P.I.—Crim. 217.20 (rev. 2023). Charged against the client on an acting-in-concert theory. Lesser included: common law robbery, N.C.P.I.—Crim. 217.10.*

| # | Element (pattern language) | State's proof in the record | Strength | Defense attack |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | unlawfully took personal property from the person or presence of another | *"He took my purse right off my shoulder"* — Jane Doe 1 (Narr. #1) | **STRONG** | — |
| 2 | without the victim's voluntary consent, by endangering or threatening her life with the use or threatened use of a firearm or other dangerous weapon | Item recovered is logged *"airsoft pistol, orange tip removed"* — property log | **NO PROOF** | No firearms examination; move to dismiss the 14-87 |
| 3 | the taker knew he was not entitled to the property | Purse recovered from **Doe 1's** waistband, not the client's | **CONTESTED** | Attribution — the client never possessed the property |
| 4 | intending to deprive the owner of its use permanently | Flight from the scene with the property (Narr. #1) | **CONTESTED** | Same attribution problem; flight is Doe 1's |

**Element 2 — NO PROOF.** Nothing in the record establishes the item's capacity
to produce death or great bodily harm; the report offers appearance only, and the
property log's own words contradict the narrative's *"handgun."* No firearms-unit
examination has been requested three months after seizure. As the record stands
the 14-87 does not reach the jury on this element, and the realistic exposure —
for both men — is common law robbery.

**Elements 3 and 4 — CONTESTED as to the client.** Both are proved, if at all,
through Doe 1's conduct. They reach the client only if the State carries acting
in concert below.

#### Acting in concert — *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 202.10*

| # | Element (pattern language) | State's proof in the record | Strength | Defense attack |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the defendant was present at the scene of the crime | *"I think that's the other one"* — Jane Doe 1 at the 06/03 show-up | **CONTESTED** | Equivocal show-up ID; no description of the second man beyond *"dark clothing"* |
| 2 | acting together with another who does the acts necessary to constitute the crime | *"run that"* — attributed by Jane Doe 1 to the shorter man (Narr. #1) | **WEAK** | Heard once, in the dark, from a man she could not identify; no other witness |
| 3 | pursuant to a common purpose to commit the crime | Narr. #1 describes the second man standing at the driver's door *"without speaking"* | **NO PROOF** | Mere presence; the record attributes no act to the client |

**Element 3 — NO PROOF.** The only conduct the report attributes to the second
man is standing still and silent. Everything else in Narr. #1 is written as *"the
suspects"* — a collective noun the narrative never unpacks. Presence plus
proximity to a codefendant is not a common purpose, and this is where the charge
against the client fails if it fails anywhere.

### Charge 2 — Conspiracy to commit robbery, G.S. 14-2.4 (Class E)
*Elements per N.C.P.I.—Crim. 202.80 (rev. 2019).*

| # | Element (pattern language) | State's proof in the record | Strength | Defense attack |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the defendant and at least one other person entered into an agreement | *"run that"* — Jane Doe 1 (Narr. #1); Doe 1's 06/11 statement that the client *"told him to do it"* | **WEAK** | Three words plus a codefendant's post-charge statement; Bruton bars the latter in a joint trial |
| 2 | to commit robbery | Same statement, immediately preceding the taking | **CONTESTED** | *"Run that"* is not self-defining; no evidence of what was said before the approach |
| 3 | the parties intended the agreement be carried out at the time it was made | Coordinated approach and joint flight (Narr. #1) | **WEAK** | Walking and running with another person is not an agreement |

**Elements 1 and 3 — WEAK.** Strip the 06/11 codefendant statement and the
conspiracy rests entirely on one witness's report of three words she attributes
to a man she could not identify, in a lot she described as unlit. Doe 1's
statement came nine days after the offense, in his second interview, on the day
he was charged — its timing is the cross.

## 3. WEAKNESSES IN THE STATE'S CASE

### Attribution — the central problem

- **The narrative never separates the two men.** After the first paragraph Narr.
  #1 uses *"the suspects"* and *"they"* throughout. **Use:** cross Ofc. Reyes
  line by line on which man each sentence describes; this is the spine of the
  acting-in-concert argument and the motion to dismiss.
- **No property, no weapon in the client's hand.** The purse was on Doe 1; the
  narrative attributes the gun to the taller man. **Use:** argue at the close of
  the State's evidence.
- **The airsoft was on the client — and contradicts the complainant.** She
  described the *taller* man as the one who displayed the item (Narr. #1); it was
  recovered from the shorter. **Use:** either the item is not what she saw, or her
  account of who did what is wrong. Both help. Do not overplay it — it is also
  the State's best fact against the client.

### Witness credibility and reliability

- **Single-witness case.** Every fact about the offense comes from Jane Doe 1.
  John Doe 3, the only neutral witness, could not identify anyone (06/04).
  **Use:** *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 104.05* (circumstantial evidence); closing.
- **No description of the client was ever given.** *"Dark clothing"* is the whole
  of it — no build, race, age, height, or face (Narr. #1; 911 log). **Use:** cross;
  §5.
- **Codefendant's statement is nine days late and same-day charged.** Doe 1 gave
  nothing on 06/03 and implicated the client on 06/11, the day he was charged
  (supplement 06/11). **Use:** *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 104.25* (accomplice testimony) if
  he testifies; demand any consideration given — Giglio.

### Investigative gaps and police work

- **Block never canvassed.** The 06/04 supplement records the 400-block
  businesses *"were not canvassed"*; no canvass appears since and retention
  windows have likely run. **Use:** preservation letter now; closing on the
  adequacy of the investigation.
- **No BWC in the production.** The log lists Ofc. Reyes as equipped; no recording
  from the stop or the show-up was produced. A show-up recording would document
  exactly what Jane Doe 1 said about the client. **Use:** discovery demand;
  sanctions motion if it was never activated or has been lost.
- **Purse never processed.** No latent-print request appears for the item at the
  center of the case. **Use:** closing; the State cannot show the client ever
  touched it.
- **No firearms examination** of the airsoft, three months after seizure (06/09).
  **Use:** element 2 of Charge 1.
- **No basis stated for the stop.** Narr. #1 offers *"matched the description"*;
  the only description broadcast for the second man was *"dark clothing."*
  **Use:** motion to suppress the stop and everything from it, including the
  show-up identification and the airsoft.

### Internal contradictions

- **Narrative vs. property log.** *"Handgun"* (Narr. #1) against *"airsoft
  pistol, orange tip removed"* (property log). **Use:** Charge 1, element 2; cross.
- **Clothing mismatch as to the codefendant.** *"An orange sweater"* (Jane Doe 1)
  against the red hoodie Doe 1 was booked in; no orange sweater recovered anywhere
  (06/03). **Use:** it undermines her observation and her identification as a
  whole, including the equivocal one of the client.

### Constitutional and suppression exposure

- **The stop.** No articulable facts documented as to the client. **Use:** motion
  to suppress (G.S. 15A-974, 15A-977 — *verify*).
- **The show-up.** Street show-up rather than an array, at 0012, of a man the
  witness had never described. **Use:** motion to suppress the identification;
  *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 104.90*; measure the procedure against the Eyewitness
  Identification Reform Act (G.S. 15A-284.52 — *verify*).
- **Bruton.** Doe 1's 06/11 statement is inadmissible against the client and
  cannot be cured by redaction if the client is the only other person described.
  **Use:** motion to sever (G.S. 15A-927 — *verify*); *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 101.42*.
- **The client's silence.** The 06/03 supplement records that he declined to
  speak. **Use:** motion in limine; *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 101.30*. Ensure it never
  reaches the jury.

### Charging and procedural

- **Value never itemized**, and no ownership documentation for the purse contents
  appears in the file. **Use:** relevant to any lesser-included larceny theory.
- **Counsel-conflict screening.** If this office also represents Doe 1, the 06/11
  statement makes the conflict immediate. **Use:** RPC 1.7 screening before the
  next setting.

*Swept and clean: chain of custody, response time, translation, officer-observed
injuries (none reported), offense date, venue, statute of limitations.*

## 4. DEFENSES

**No affirmative defense is supported by this record**, and the analysis should
not manufacture one. Swept and not reached: self-defense and defense of others
(*308.40 / 308.45 / 308.50*) — no force is attributed to the client at all;
accident (*307.11*); duress (*310.10*) — nothing in the record suggests the
client acted under threat, though this is a question for the client interview,
not for the report; alibi (*301.10*) — the client was stopped four blocks from
the scene eleven minutes after the call; entrapment, intoxication, and capacity —
no supporting facts documented.

The client's real defense is not an affirmative one. It is **mere presence**: the
State's own narrative places him standing silent at a car door and attributes no
act to him, and its bridge to Doe 1's conduct (*N.C.P.I.—Crim. 202.10*) fails on
common purpose. That is an attack on the State's proof, not a defense the client
carries — nothing shifts to him and no instruction burden attaches. Keep the two
separate at the charge conference.

**Develop:** the client interview is the gate on duress and on any account of the
walk east; the BWC and the show-up recording are the gate on what Jane Doe 1
actually said about him.

## 5. IDENTIFICATION

The State ties the client to the offense by proximity in time and place, the
airsoft recovered from him, and Jane Doe 1's equivocal show-up statement — *"I
think that's the other one"* (06/03). There is no other identification of him in
the file.

Against that: **she never described him** beyond *"dark clothing"*, so there is
no description to match; the procedure was a **street show-up** at 0012, not an
array, of a man in custody beside the man she had just identified as the gunman;
she described the lot as unlit (*"the lot lights were out"*); John Doe 3's photo
array on 06/04 produced no identification of anyone; and her *"orange sweater"*
description of the codefendant did not match his booking clothing. The array
materials and any BWC of the show-up are not in the production.

**Instructions to request:** *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 104.90*; *104.98* if the 06/04 array
is used. **Consider** an eyewitness-identification expert if the case is tried.

## 6. BOTTOM LINE

| Charge | State's proof | Defense posture |
|---|---|---|
| Robbery w/ dangerous weapon, G.S. 14-87 (acting in concert) | **Weapon element: NO PROOF; common purpose: NO PROOF** | *Move to dismiss; the client's exposure on this record is nothing* |
| Conspiracy to commit robbery, G.S. 14-2.4 | **Weak — three words and a late codefendant statement** | *Sever first, then try the sufficiency of the agreement* |

The best issue is attribution: the State's own narrative gives the client no act
beyond standing at a car door. Sever before anything else — Doe 1's 06/11
statement is the only evidence of agreement that does not depend on Jane Doe 1,
and it is inadmissible against the client in a joint trial. If the 14-87 falls on
the weapon element, the realistic exposure for both men is common law robbery,
and for the client only if acting in concert is carried. The stop itself has no
documented basis as to the client, and the suppression motion should be filed
before the identification issue is litigated.
