# Weakness Checklists

The defense-side sweep. The cross-cutting checklists run on **every** case; the
charge-specific ones run whenever the matching offense (or an analogous one) is
analyzed.

**These are checks you run, not sections you write.** Run every applicable item
against the record — then put only the **hits** in the draft. An item that came
up clean earns no sentence, no "not applicable," no reassurance that it was
considered; the one-line "swept and clean" close in Section 3 covers all of them
at once.

**A hit is reported in one or two lines, in the report's own words, and it ends
in a use.** Bold label, the problem, the source, **Use:**. *"An orange sweater"
(Doe 1) vs. the red hoodie booked at arrest (Narr. #1). **Use:** cross; §5 ID
attack* does the work of a paragraph.

**Authority flagged *(verify)* below is outside the local libraries.** This
plugin ships G.S. Chapter 14 and the N.C.P.I.—Crim. instructions. Chapter 15A
procedure, Chapter 90 drug offenses, Chapter 20 motor vehicle offenses, the
Rules of Evidence, and all case law are **not** shipped. Every such cite is a
pointer for counsel to confirm — never state a holding from memory, and never
let an unverified cite reach a filing.

## Cross-cutting sweep (every case)

### Witness credibility and reliability

- [ ] **Prior inconsistent statements.** Compare each witness's account across
      every narrative, supplement, 911 transcript, and interview. Any change in
      the account — however small — is a cross point. *Instruction:*
      *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 105.20* (impeachment or corroboration by prior statement).
- [ ] **Interest and bias.** Is the witness a charged codefendant, a cooperating
      witness, an informant, someone with pending charges, a family member of the
      complainant, or a party to a civil claim arising from the same events?
      *Instructions:* *104.20* (interested witness), *104.25* (accomplice
      testimony for the prosecution), *104.30* (informer or undercover agent).
- [ ] **Opportunity to observe.** Distance, duration, lighting, obstruction,
      angle, whether the witness's attention was on a weapon. Pull the report's
      own words on all of it.
- [ ] **Condition of the witness.** Intoxication, injury, fear, medication, age,
      how long after the event the statement was taken.
- [ ] **Statement provenance.** Was the statement recorded, written by the
      witness, or only paraphrased by an officer in a narrative? A witness
      account that exists solely as an officer's summary is attackable as the
      officer's words, not the witness's.
- [ ] **Delay and motive.** Delay in reporting; a report made only after some
      other event (an arrest, a custody dispute, a claim); any documented history
      between the witness and the client.
- [ ] **The complainant's own conduct.** Where the record shows the complainant
      as an initiator, an aggressor, or an armed participant, that is both a
      credibility fact and a self-defense fact — carry it to Section 4.

### Investigative gaps and police work

- [ ] **Steps not taken.** Canvass not done, leads not followed, a named witness
      never interviewed, a suspect vehicle never located, a follow-up assigned
      and never closed. Each one is a closing argument.
- [ ] **Evidence not collected or not tested.** Swabs never submitted, prints
      never compared, a phone never extracted, clothing never seized, a weapon
      never examined. Non-collection is often stronger than a negative result.
- [ ] **Body-worn and dashboard camera.** Was BWC activated? When — before or
      after the contact? Is the full recording in the production, or only the
      clip the narrative describes? Missing or late-activated BWC is both a
      weakness and a discovery demand.
- [ ] **Report timing and authorship.** A narrative written days or weeks after
      the event, a supplement with no author, a report that adopts another
      officer's account without saying so.
- [ ] **Conclusory narrative language.** *"Resisted," "furtive movement," "in
      plain view," "consented," "appeared impaired," "serious injury"* — each is
      a conclusion. Ask what observed fact is offered for it. Where none is,
      that is a hit.
- [ ] **Single-source corroboration.** Where the entire case rests on one
      officer's or one witness's account with nothing independent, say so
      plainly. *Instruction:* *104.05* (circumstantial evidence) where the proof
      is inferential.
- [ ] **Chain of custody.** Gaps between seizure, logging, and submission;
      property-log entries that do not match the narrative; items logged by
      someone who never appears in a narrative.
- [ ] **Translation and interpretation.** A statement taken through a bystander,
      a family member, an app, or an officer without certification.
- [ ] **Scene documentation.** No photographs, no measurements, no diagram, a
      scene released before processing.

### Physical and forensic evidence

- [ ] **What was never recovered** — weapon, stolen property, casings, clothing,
      the vehicle. Non-recovery goes in Section 3 with the element it undercuts.
- [ ] **Where the sample came from.** DNA on the *outside* of a vehicle proves
      contact with the outside of a vehicle. State the location precisely and
      say what it does not establish.
- [ ] **Forensic limits.** DNA mixtures and touch-DNA transfer; partial or
      low-quality latents; the absence of a quantitative conclusion in the
      report; whether the analyst's bench notes were produced. Consider whether
      an independent expert is warranted — that is a Section 7 item.
- [ ] **Surveillance.** Does it exist? Was it collected before the retention
      window closed? Does it actually show the contested fact (the face, the
      shot, the direction of travel) or only that someone was present?
- [ ] **Lab reports absent from the production.** A referenced report that is not
      produced is a discovery demand, not a fact gap.
- [ ] **Injury severity.** Never accept "serious injury" as documented. Look for
      wound description, treatment, hospitalization, lasting impairment. Absent
      those facts it is a **NO PROOF** or **WEAK** element, not a quibble.

### Identification integrity

- [ ] **Description vs. appearance at arrest.** Compare every witness description
      (clothing, build, race, age, height, hair, facial hair, tattoos) against
      the client's documented appearance at apprehension. Flag every mismatch.
      In the study's control case, an *"orange sweater"* description against a
      red hoodie at arrest was caught by all six evaluating prosecutors — assume
      opposing counsel already knows about the one in this file.
- [ ] **Procedure used.** Show-up, photo array, live lineup, or spontaneous
      identification. **Name show-up suggestibility explicitly whenever a show-up
      occurred** — it is the most attackable procedure in routine use.
- [ ] **Eyewitness Identification Reform Act compliance** (G.S. 15A-284.52 —
      *verify*). Independent administrator; the filler composition; the
      instructions given; whether the witness's confidence statement was recorded
      at the time; whether the procedure was documented. Noncompliance supports
      both a suppression motion and *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 104.98* (photo lineup
      requirements) at trial.
- [ ] **Failed and non-identifications.** Search every narrative and supplement
      for a witness who did not identify anyone, identified someone else, or was
      uncertain. Report every one — a neutral witness's non-identification is
      among the strongest defense facts a file can contain.
- [ ] **Viewing conditions.** Lighting (*"dimly lit area"*), distance, duration,
      *"did not get a good look at the suspect's face,"* elapsed time between the
      event and the identification, weapon focus.
- [ ] **Cross-racial identification** where the record shows it.
- [ ] **Contamination.** Did witnesses view together, speak to each other, see a
      social-media post or news photo, or hear the suspect described over the
      radio before viewing?
- [ ] *Instruction:* *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 104.90* (identification of a defendant as
      perpetrator) in every contested-ID case.

### Report inconsistencies

- [ ] **Cross-check descriptions** of people, vehicles, and property across every
      narrative. Where they conflict (white vs. grey/silver Nissan), report the
      conflict — never silently adopt one version.
- [ ] **Narrative vs. log.** A *"handgun"* in the narrative and an *"airsoft
      pistol"* on the property log is a case-dispositive contradiction, not a
      clerical one.
- [ ] **Timeline.** Check dispatch, arrival, stop, and identification times
      against each other and against distances. Timeline impossibilities are
      quantitative and hard for a witness to talk around.
- [ ] **Counts vs. charging language.** Does the number of charged counts match
      the number of victims, structures, or transactions the report describes?
- [ ] **Disorganized or incomplete reporting** is itself case information — say
      so.

### Statements

- [ ] **Quote legally significant statements verbatim** — the client's, the
      codefendants', the witnesses'. Colloquial phrases can carry elements
      (*"run that"*).
- [ ] **Admission vs. confession.** *"Admitted to knowing the truck was stolen"*
      and *"admitted to being in the truck"* are admissions of circumstantial
      facts, not confessions — no matter what the narrative calls them. Correct
      the characterization in the analysis. *Instructions:* *104.60*
      (admissions), *104.70* (confessions).
- [ ] **Custody and interrogation.** Was the client in custody? Was there
      interrogation or its functional equivalent? Is a *Miranda* warning
      documented, with time and waiver? Was the statement electronically recorded
      (G.S. 15A-211 — *verify*)? An unrecorded custodial statement in a case
      where recording was required is a suppression issue.
- [ ] **Voluntariness.** Length of the interview, the client's condition,
      promises or threats documented in the narrative, whether counsel was
      requested.
- [ ] **Codefendant statements.** Any codefendant statement implicating the
      client raises *Bruton* and a severance motion (G.S. 15A-927 — *verify*),
      and *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 101.42* at trial.
- [ ] **Silence.** Ensure the analysis never treats the client's silence or
      refusal to consent as evidence of anything. *Instruction:* *101.30*.

### Constitutional and suppression exposure

Each hit names the **motion** and the **ground**. Supporting authority is flagged
*(verify)* and confirmed by counsel before filing.

- [ ] **The stop.** What specific, articulable facts are documented for the stop —
      not the officer's conclusion? A traffic stop's stated basis; whether the
      violation is actually a violation; how long the stop lasted before it
      became something else.
- [ ] **The frisk.** Documented facts supporting a belief the client was armed
      and dangerous, distinct from the basis for the stop.
- [ ] **The search.** Warrant, consent, plain view, search incident to arrest,
      automobile exception, inventory pursuant to a written policy, exigency.
      Which is claimed, and what documented fact supports it?
- [ ] **Consent.** Who gave it, when, in what words, whether it was recorded,
      whether the person had authority, and whether it followed an unlawful
      detention.
- [ ] **Warrants.** Is the application in the production? Does the affidavit
      establish probable cause on its face? Does the description match what was
      searched? Is the return filed?
- [ ] **The arrest.** Probable cause for the offense actually charged; whether
      the arrest preceded or followed the search.
- [ ] ***Brady* / *Giglio*.** Anything in the record suggesting exculpatory
      material, an unproduced witness statement, or impeachment material on a
      testifying officer. Demand it in Section 7.
- [ ] **Discovery compliance** (G.S. 15A-903, 15A-904, 15A-910 — *verify*). Note
      that statutory open-file discovery attaches in superior court; district
      court misdemeanor practice differs — *verify* the current rule before
      relying on it.
- [ ] **Suppression procedure** (G.S. 15A-971 through 15A-980, including the
      motion-and-affidavit requirement and pretrial timing under G.S. 15A-952 —
      *verify*). Note the deadline consequence in Section 7.

### Charging and procedural defects

- [ ] **Variance.** Does the charging language match the conduct the report
      describes — the right victim, the right property, the right date, the right
      subsection?
- [ ] **Facial sufficiency** of the warrant, citation, or indictment; a missing
      essential element on the face of the pleading (G.S. 15A-924, 15A-954 —
      *verify*).
- [ ] **Offense date** consistent across the charging documents and the report.
- [ ] **Jurisdiction and venue** — county, and whether any element occurred
      outside North Carolina. *Instruction:* *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 311.10* (lack of
      jurisdiction, with special verdict form).
- [ ] **Statute of limitations** on misdemeanors (*verify* the current period).
- [ ] **Joinder and severance** (G.S. 15A-926, 15A-927 — *verify*): unrelated
      offenses joined; codefendants whose defenses are antagonistic.
- [ ] **Merger and double jeopardy.** Two counts arising from a single act, or a
      lesser included charged alongside the greater — flag for counsel to
      research.
- [ ] **Speedy trial and pretrial custody posture**, including bond conditions
      worth revisiting.
- [ ] **Client status elements.** Where an element depends on a prior conviction,
      a prior order, or a status (felon in possession, DVPO violation, habitual
      status), confirm the record actually documents it. These elements are
      routinely assumed and frequently unproven.

### Defenses trigger sweep

- [ ] Any claim of **self-defense, accident, alibi, duress, necessity,
      entrapment, or intoxication** anywhere in the record triggers full analysis
      against the pattern instruction — see `defenses-catalog.md`.
- [ ] **Physical positions matter.** The client on the ground *under* the
      complainant is a self-defense fact even if no one uses the words.
- [ ] **Accident and self-defense are separate** — address each on its own.
- [ ] **Mere presence** wherever the client's link to the offense is proximity or
      association rather than conduct.
- [ ] **Third-party guilt** where the record identifies another person with
      motive, opportunity, or matching description — flag it, and flag that its
      admissibility must be researched *(verify)*.

## Charge-specific checklists

**Robbery with a dangerous weapon — G.S. 14-87 (N.C.P.I. 217.20/217.30)**
- [ ] The dangerous weapon must be **proven**, not described. An airsoft pistol,
      BB gun, replica, or an item never recovered is a live **NO PROOF** argument
      on that element. Where it fails, the exposure is common law robbery
      (*N.C.P.I. 217.10*) — say so.
- [ ] Intent to permanently deprive is a separate element. Where the property was
      abandoned, returned, or the taking followed a dispute over ownership, that
      element is in play.
- [ ] Life endangered or threatened: tie it to specific documented conduct, not
      to the presence of the weapon.
- [ ] Taking *from the person or presence*: check the actual distance and timing.

**Discharging a firearm into occupied property — G.S. 14-34.1 (N.C.P.I. 208.90)**
- [ ] **Occupancy at the time of discharge** must be established per structure or
      vehicle. A parked car is not occupied without a fact saying so — this is
      the most commonly unproven element in the offense.
- [ ] Knowledge or reasonable grounds to believe occupancy — a separate element
      with separate proof.
- [ ] Each structure or vehicle is a separate count; check whether the counts
      match the record.

**AWDW / AWDWIKISI — G.S. 14-32, 14-33 (N.C.P.I. 208.10 et seq.)**
- [ ] **Serious injury** is a fact question — see the injury item above. Absent
      documented severity it is **WEAK** at best.
- [ ] **Intent to kill** needs facts beyond the assault itself (multiple shots,
      close range, statements, pursuit). Where those are absent, the greater
      offense is vulnerable and the lesser is the realistic exposure.
- [ ] **Deadly weapon character** of the object used (hands? a shod foot? a
      vehicle?) — a fact question where the object is not per se deadly.
- [ ] Self-defense is in play in most assault cases; run the trigger sweep.

**Assault on a female — G.S. 14-33(c)(2)**
- [ ] A male defendant **at least 18 years old** is an element. If the client's
      age is not documented in the record, it is **NO PROOF** — not a formality.

**Assault by pointing a gun — G.S. 14-34 (N.C.P.I. 208.85)**
- [ ] The elements are (1) the defendant pointed a gun at the person, (2)
      intentionally. *"In fun or otherwise"* means the reason is irrelevant — it
      is **not** an element. Loaded or unloaded is irrelevant. Do not build a
      defense on either.

**Possession offenses — G.S. 90-95, firearm by felon, etc.**
- [ ] **Attribution.** Exactly where was the item found, and where was **the
      client** in relation to it? In a shared vehicle or residence, this is the
      whole case.
- [ ] **Actual vs. constructive possession** — control over the place where the
      item was found, and whether the client had exclusive control.
      *Instruction:* *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 104.41* (actual-constructive possession).
- [ ] **Multi-occupant spaces.** Who else had access? Non-exclusive control
      requires incriminating circumstances beyond presence.
- [ ] **The predicate.** For firearm-by-felon, is the prior conviction actually
      documented in the production?
- [ ] **Identity and weight of the substance** — field test vs. lab
      confirmation; whether the lab report is in the production; whether weight
      determines the offense class.
- [ ] Chapter 90 is not in the local statute library — quote the charging
      language and flag *(verify)*.

**Conspiracy — N.C.P.I. 202.80**
- [ ] The agreement is the element. Parallel presence is not agreement. Name the
      specific facts the State offers for a meeting of the minds, and attack the
      gap where the proof is only that two people were in the same place.
- [ ] Where the only proof of agreement is a codefendant's statement, the
      *Bruton* and severance issues run together with the sufficiency issue.

**Acting in concert — N.C.P.I. 202.10**
- [ ] Presence **plus** a common purpose **plus** acting in furtherance. Where
      the record shows only presence, mere presence is the defense and this is
      usually the strongest issue in a multi-defendant case.
- [ ] Check whether the State's theory is acting in concert at all, or whether it
      is charging the client with the principal's conduct without saying how.

**Resisting, delaying, or obstructing — G.S. 14-223**
- [ ] The officer must have been **discharging a lawful duty** — if the
      underlying stop or arrest was unlawful, the element fails.
- [ ] Identify the specific acts alleged as resistance. Verbal objection,
      questioning, and passive non-cooperation are frequently charged and
      frequently insufficient.

**Larceny and breaking or entering — G.S. 14-54, 14-72**
- [ ] Ownership and value: is value documented, or assumed? Value determines the
      class.
- [ ] Intent at the time of entry is a separate element from what was taken
      afterward.
- [ ] Where the case rests on possession of the property afterward, check the
      doctrine of recent possession and its timing requirement. *Instruction:*
      *N.C.P.I.—Crim. 104.40*.

**Impaired driving — G.S. 20-138.1** *(Chapter 20 is not in the local statute
library — verify everything here)*
- [ ] Basis for the stop; the checkpoint's written plan and supervisory approval
      if applicable.
- [ ] Operation: is there a documented fact that the client drove?
- [ ] Field sobriety administration and whether the officer is certified.
- [ ] Chemical analysis: the instrument, the operator's permit, the observation
      period, the affidavit, and whether the implied-consent rights were given in
      writing and orally with the time documented.
- [ ] Any documented denial of access to a witness during the observation period.
