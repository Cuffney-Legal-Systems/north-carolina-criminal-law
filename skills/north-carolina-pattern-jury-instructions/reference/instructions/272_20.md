272.20 TRANSPORTATION WITHIN PASSENGER AREA OF MOTOR VEHICLE OF
FORTIFIED WINE OR SPIRITUOUS LIQUOR IN OTHER THAN MANUFACTURER'S
UNOPENED ORIGINAL CONTAINER. MISDEMEANOR.

The defendant has been charged with transporting within the passenger

area  of  a  motor  vehicle1  [fortified  wine]  [spirituous  liquor]  in  other  than  the

manufacturer's unopened original container.2

For you to find the defendant guilty of this offense the State must prove

four things beyond a reasonable doubt:

First,  that  the  defendant  was  driving  a  motor  vehicle  on  a  public

highway.3

Second,  that  a  container  was  within  the  passenger  area  of  the  motor

vehicle, and that it contained [fortified wine]4 [spirituous liquor].5

Third,  that  the  beverage  was  not  in  the  manufacturer's  unopened

original  container.  A  container  with  the  seal  broken  is  opened  within  the

meaning of the statute.)6

And Fourth, that the defendant knew he was transporting that container

in the passenger area of the motor vehicle.7

If  you  find  from  the  evidence  beyond  a  reasonable  doubt  that  on  or

about  the  alleged  date  the  defendant  drove  a  motor  vehicle  on  a  public

highway,  that  a  container  within  the  passenger  area  of  the  motor  vehicle

contained  [fortified  wine]  [spirituous

liquor]  which  was  not

in  the

manufacturer's unopened original container, and that the defendant knew that

he was transporting that opened container in the passenger area of the motor

vehicle,  it  would  be  your  duty  to  return  a  verdict  of  guilty.  If  you  do  not  so

find or have a reasonable doubt as to one or more of these things, it would be

your duty to return a verdict of not guilty.

1. “Passenger area of a motor vehicle” is defined in G.S. 18B-401(c). “Motor vehicle” is

defined in G.S. 20-4.01(23).

2. G.S. 18B-401(b) provides, “It shall be unlawful for a person operating a for-hire

passenger vehicle as defined in G.S. 20-4.01(27){b), to transport fortified wine or spirituous
liquor unless the vehicle is transporting a paying passenger who owns the alcoholic beverage
being transported (emphasis added).”

3. Public highway is defined in G.S. 20-4.01.

4. G.S. 18B-101(7) defines fortified wine.

5. G.S. 18B-101(14) defines spirituous liquor.

6. G.S. 18B-401 (c).

7. Guilty knowledge is essential for conviction for illegally transporting intoxicating

liquors, and this section (G.S. 18B-401) will be interpreted in light of the common law
principle that guilty knowledge is an essential element of crime. State v. Welch, 232 N.C. 77,
59 S.E.2d 199 (1950).
