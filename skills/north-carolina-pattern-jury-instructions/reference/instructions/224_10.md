224.10 [WILLFUL] [MALICIOUS] INJURY TO [RENTED] [HIRED] PERSONAL
PROPERTY. MISDEMEANOR. G.S. § 14-165.1

The  defendant  has  been  charged  with  willfully  or  maliciously  injuring

[rented] [hired] personal property.

For  you  to  find  the  defendant  guilty  of  this  offense,  the  State  must

prove four things beyond a reasonable doubt:

First, that the defendant [rented] [hired] (describe personal property)

from the victim.

Second, that the defendant [injured] [damaged] the property.

Third,  that  he  did  this  willfully,  that  is,  intentionally  and  without

justification or excuse; (or maliciously, that is, with animosity, hatred, or ill

will.)

And  Fourth,  that  he  did  this  by  [using]  [driving]  the  property  in

violation of a law of North Carolina. (Name and summarize law violated) is a

law of North Carolina.

If  you  find  from  the  evidence  beyond  a  reasonable  doubt  that  on  or

about the alleged date the defendant had [rented] [hired] (describe personal

property)  from  the  victim,  and  the  defendant,  willfully  (or  maliciously)

[injured]  [damaged]  the  property  while  [using]  (or)  [driving]  it  in  violation

of (name or describe law), it would be your duty to return a verdict of guilty.

If you do not so find or have a reasonable doubt as to one or more of these

things, it would be your duty to return a verdict of not guilty.

1. The statute punishes personally caused injury as well as permitting others to
cause injury. This instruction assumes the former (personally causing injury), but with
minor modifications can be adapted to the latter.
