214.72 BUYING TIMBER DIRECTLY FROM THE OWNER AND FAILING TO
MAKE PAYMENT IN FULL.

The defendant has been charged with buying timber directly from the

owner and failing to make payment in full.1

For you to find the defendant guilty of this offense, the State must prove

two things beyond a reasonable doubt:

First, that the defendant bought timber directly from the owner of the

timber.

And Second, that the defendant failed to make payment  in full to the

owner [by the date specified in the written timber sales agreement] [within

60 days from the date that the buyer removes the timber from the property].

(Evidence that the defendant failed to make payment in full within 10

days after the mailing or personal delivery of the written  notification of  the

owner’s demand for payment shall constitute prima facie evidence of intent.

You may, but are not compelled to, find the defendant intentionally failed to

make payment in full to the owner.)

If  you  find  from  the  evidence  beyond  a  reasonable  doubt  that  on  or

about the alleged date, the defendant bought timber directly from the owner

of the timber and failed to make payment in full  to the owner by [the date

specified in the written timber sales agreement] [60 days from the date that

the buyer removes the timber from the property] (nothing else appearing), it

would be your duty to return a verdict of guilty. If you do not so find or if you

have a reasonable doubt as to one or more of these things, it would be your

duty to return a verdict of not guilty.2
