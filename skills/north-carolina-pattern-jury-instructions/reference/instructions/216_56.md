216.56. LARCENY FROM A MERCHANT.1 FELONY.

The  defendant  has  been  charged  with  felonious  larceny  from  a

merchant.

For you to find the defendant guilty of this offense, the State must prove

three things beyond a reasonable doubt:

First, that the defendant committed larceny. Larceny is the taking and

carrying away of the personal property of another without consent with the

intent to deprive the owner of possession permanently.2

Second, that the defendant committed larceny by: (Choose one of the

following.)

a)  [taking property valued at more than $200 by exiting the premises

of the store through an emergency exit door.3]

b)  [removing]  [destroying]  [deactivating]  a  component  of  an

[antishoplifting] [inventory control device]4 to precent activation of

any [antishoplifting] [inventory control device].]

c)  [taking infant formula valued in excess of $100.5]

d)  [exchanging  property  for  cash,  a  gift  card,  a  merchandise  card,  or

some other item of value, knowing or having reasonable grounds to

believe the property is stolen.]

And Third, that the victim was a merchant.

If  you  find  from  the  evidence  beyond  a  reasonable  doubt  that  on  or

about  the  alleged  date,  the  defendant  took  and  carried  away  the  personal

property  of  a  merchant  without  consent  and  with  intent  to  deprive  the

merchant of possession permanently by

b)  [removing]  [destroying]  [deactivating]  a  component  of  an

it would be your duty to return a verdict of guilty. If you do not so find or have

a reasonable doubt as to one or more of these things it would be your duty to

return a verdict of not guilty.

1. N.C. Gen. Stat. § 25-2-104 defines “merchant” as “a person who deals in goods of
the kind or otherwise by his occupation holds himself out as having knowledge of skill peculiar
to the practices or goods involved in the transaction or to whom such knowledge or skill may
be  attributed  by  his  employment  of  an  agent  or  broker  or  other  intermediary  who  by  his
occupation holds himself out as having such knowledge or skill.”

2. See State v. Elliot, 21 N.C. App. 555 (1974).

3. See 29 C.F.R. § 1910.36-.37.

4.  The  phrase  “antishoplifting  or  inventory  control  device”  means  any  physical  or
electronic mechanism utilized to prevent larceny from a merchant, including, but not limited
to, product security tags affixed to an item, shelving, security cameras, and security systems
utilized by a merchant to prevent larceny. See N.C. Gen. Stat. § 14-72.11(2).

5. The term “infant formula” means a food, which purports to be or is represented for
special dietary use solely as a food for infants by reason of its simulation of human milk or its
sustainability as a complete or partial substitute for human milk. See 21 U.S.C. § 321(z).
