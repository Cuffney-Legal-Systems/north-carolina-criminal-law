208.75  ASSAULT ON A CHILD UNDER THE AGE OF TWELVE YEARS.
MISDEMEANOR.

The defendant has been charged with assault1 on a child under the

age of twelve years.

For you to find the defendant guilty of this offense, the State must

prove two things beyond a reasonable doubt:

First, that the defendant intentionally2 (and without justification or

excuse3)4 assaulted the victim by (describe assault).

And Second, that the victim had not reached his twelfth birthday at

the time the assault was committed.

NOTE WELL:  If self-defense is an issue, use N.C.P.I.-
Crim. 308.40.

If you find from the evidence beyond a reasonable doubt that on or

about  the  alleged  date,  the  defendant  intentionally  (describe  assault)

and that at that time the victim had not yet reached his twelfth birthday,

(nothing  else  appearing)5  it  would  be  your  duty  to  return  a  verdict  of

guilty.  If you do not  so  find  or  have  a  reasonable  doubt  as  to  one  or

more  of  these  things,  it  would  be  your  duty  to  return  a  verdict  of  not

guilty.6

NOTE WELL:  If self-defense is an issue, use mandate
from N.C.P.I.-Crim. 308.40.7

2. If a definition of intent is needed, see N.C.P.I.-Crim. 120.10.
