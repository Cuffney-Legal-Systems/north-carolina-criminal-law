237.60 POSSESSION OF ILLEGAL SLOT MACHINE. MISDEMEANOR.

The  defendant  has  been  charged  with  possessing  an  illegal  slot

machine.

For  you  to  find  the  defendant  guilty  of  this  offense,  the  State  must

prove two things beyond a reasonable doubt:

First, that the defendant possessed an illegal slot machine.

An  illegal  slot  machine  is  one  that  is  designed  not  to  produce  for,  or

give  to  the  person  who  places  a  coin  or  money  (or  the  representative  of

either), the same return in market value each and every time such machine

is operated.1 A person possesses (describe machine) when he is aware of its

presence, and has (either by himself or together with others) both the power

and intent to control its disposition or use.

NOTE WELL: If constructive possession of the illegal slot machine
is  an  issue,  or  an  amplified  definition  of  actual  possession  is
needed, the trial judge should refer to N.C.P.I.—Crim. 104.41 for
further instructions.

And  Second,  the  defendant  must  have  possessed  the  illegal  slot

machine for the purpose that it be operated as an illegal slot machine.

If  you  find  from  the  evidence  beyond  a  reasonable  doubt  that  on  or

about  the alleged date, the defendant  possessed an illegal slot  machine for

the purpose of its being operated as an illegal slot machine, then it would be

your duty to return a verdict of guilty. If you do not so find or if you have a

reasonable doubt as to one or more of these things, it would be your duty to

return a verdict of not guilty.

1. See 14-296, 14-306 for definition.
