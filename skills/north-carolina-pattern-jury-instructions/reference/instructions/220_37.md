220.37. FRAUDULENT ADVERTISEMENT OF RESIDENTIAL REAL PROPERTY.
FELONY.

The defendant has been charged with the fraudulent advertisement for

sale of residential real property.1

For you to find the defendant guilty of this offense, the State must prove

two things beyond a reasonable doubt:

First, that the defendant  [listed] [advertised] residential  real property

for sale to another person. (Describe property) is residential real property.

And Second, that the defendant did so knowing that the purported seller

had no [legal title] [authority] to sell the property.

If  you  find  from  the  evidence  beyond  a  reasonable  doubt  that  on  or

about  the  alleged  date,  the  defendant  [listed]  [advertised]  (describe

residential real property) for sale to another person, and the defendant did so

knowing  that  the  purported  seller  had  no  [legal  title]  [authority]  to  sell  the

property, it would be your duty to return a verdict of guilty. If you do not so

find or have a reasonable doubt as to one or more of these things, it would be

your duty to return a verdict of not guilty.

1.  N.C.  Gen.  Stat.  §  14-118.11  defines  “residential  real  property”  as  “real  property
located in the State of North Carolina upon which there is located or is to be located a structure
or  structures  designed  principally  for  residential  purposes,  including,  but  not  limited  to,
individual units of townhouses, condominiums, and cooperatives.”
