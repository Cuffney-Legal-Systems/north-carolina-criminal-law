101.39  LACK OF UNANIMITY ON PRINCIPAL CHARGE; "REASONABLE
EFFORTS".

NOTE  WELL:  Only  provide  this  instruction  if  this
question is raised.

With  respect  to  your  question  -  whether  the  jury  is  required

unanimously  to  find  that  the  defendant  is  not  guilty  of  first-degree

murder before considering whether he is guilty of second-degree murder?

-  I instruct you that  the  jury  should  first  consider  first  degree  murder,

but you are not required to determine unanimously that the defendant is

not  guilty  of  that  offense  before  you  may  consider  second  degree

murder.1   If  you  reach  a  unanimous  verdict  that  the  defendant  is  not

guilty of first-degree murder,  or  if,  after  all  reasonable  efforts,  you  are

unable  to  reach  a  verdict  as  to  that  offense,  then  you  may  consider

whether the defendant is guilty of second-degree murder.2

1  If  applicable,  this  instruction  should  be  modified  to  include  other  lesser-included

offenses.

2 See State v. Mays, 158 N.C. App. 563, 575, 582 S.E.2d 360, 368, disc. rev. denied,

357 N.C. 510, 588 S.E.2d 379 (2003).
