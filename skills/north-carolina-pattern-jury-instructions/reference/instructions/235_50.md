235.50  TERRORISM (BASIC OFFENSE). FELONY.

The defendant has been charged with the offense of terrorism.

For  you  to  find  defendant  guilty  of  this  offense,  the  State  must

prove two things beyond a reasonable doubt:

First,  that  the  defendant  committed  (name  offense),  an  act  of

violence.1 A person commits (name offense) when (set forth elements of

named offense using applicable Pattern Jury Instructions).

And Second, that the defendant did so with the intent to intimidate

[the  civilian  population  at  large]  [an  identifiable  group  of  the  civilian

population]] [influence through  intimidation  the  [conduct]  [activities]  of

the  government  of  [the  United  States]  [a  state]  [any  unit  of  local

government]].2

If you find from the evidence beyond a reasonable doubt that on or

about the alleged date the defendant committed (name offense), an act

of  violence,  with  the  intent  to  [intimidate  [the  civilian  population  at

large] [an identifiable group of the civilian population]] [influence through

intimidation the [conduct] [activities]  of  the  government  of  [the  United

States] [a state] [any unit of local government]], it would be your duty to

return a verdict of guilty. If you do not so find or have a reasonable doubt

as to one or more of these things, then it would be your duty to return a

verdict of not guilty.

1. N.C. Gen. Stat.   14-10.1 states that an !act of violence" means a violation of
N.C. Gen. Stat.   14-17; a felony punishable pursuant to  N.C.  Gen.  Stat.     14-18;  any
