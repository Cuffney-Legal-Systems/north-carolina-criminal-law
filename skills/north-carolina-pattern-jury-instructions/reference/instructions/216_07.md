The defendant has been charged with larceny of motor fuel1 valued

at less than $1,000.2

For you to find the defendant guilty of this offense, the State must

prove five things beyond a reasonable doubt:

First, that the defendant  took3 motor fuel belonging to  the  victim

which was valued at less than $1,000.

Second, that the defendant carried away4 the motor fuel.

Third,  that  the  victim  did  not  consent  to  the  taking  and  carrying

away of the motor fuel.

Fourth, that at the time of the taking, the defendant intended5 to

steal the motor fuel and to deprive the victim of its use permanently.6

And Fifth, that the defendant knew the defendant was not entitled7

to take the motor fuel.

about the alleged date, the defendant took and carried away the victim's

1. Motor fuel includes gasoline, diesel fuel, and blended fuel. See N.C. Gen. Stat.

105-449.60(20).

2. See N.C.P.I.-Crim. 216.10 for larcenies involving goods worth $1,000 or more.

3. If the property was severed from the possession of the victim and was under the
control of the defendant for  any  period  of  time,  even  if  only  for  an  instant,  this  would
constitute a taking.  See S. v. Carswell, 296 N.C. 101 (1978).

4. In the event that there is some dispute as to asportation, the jury should be told

that the slightest movement is sufficient.

5. See N.C.P.I.-Crim. 120.10 if a further definition of intent is needed.

6. In the event there is some dispute as to permanent deprivation, the jury should
be told that a temporary deprivation will not suffice.   But  cf. S. v. Smith, 268 N.C.  167
(1966).

7. In the event that the defendant relies on claim of right, the jury should be told
that  if  the  defendant  honestly  believed  that  he  was  entitled  to  take  the  property,  he
cannot be guilty of larceny.  Perkins & Boyce, CRIMINAL LAW, 3d Ed. (1982), at 326.
