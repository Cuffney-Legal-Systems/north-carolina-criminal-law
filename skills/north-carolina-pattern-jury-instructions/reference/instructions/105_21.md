The State contends (and the defendant denies) that the defendant

made false, contradictory, or conflicting statements.  If you find that the

defendant made such statements, they may  be  considered  by  you  as  a

circumstance tending to reflect the mental process of a person possessed

of  a  guilty  conscience,  seeking  to  divert  suspicion  or  to  exculpate  the

person, and you should consider that evidence, along with all  the  other

believable evidence in this case.  However, if you find that the defendant

made such statements,  they  do  not  create  a  presumption  of  guilt,  and

such evidence standing alone is not sufficient to  establish  guilt.    (Such

evidence may not  be  considered  as  tending  to  show  premeditation  and

deliberation.)
