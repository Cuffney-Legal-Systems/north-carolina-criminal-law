# Worked Example — Target Length and Format

**Fictional case, invented facts.** This exists to show the *shape and density*
of a finished summary, not to supply law or facts. Two charges, roughly 1,200
words of prose plus two element tables.

Notice the proportions: **Section 1 is the largest section in the document.** It
carries the whole narrative record — incident, then a dated investigative
chronology including the steps that produced nothing — so that Sections 2–6 can
be verdicts and problems rather than re-narration. They refer back by date or
short tag ("the clothing mismatch," "the 06/04 array"), never by restating.

**Match the density, not the word count.** A four-charge case runs longer, but
not proportionally — the Summary, Weaknesses, Defenses, and Identification
sections barely grow, while each added charge brings its own table and up to
~150 words of prose. If your draft says as much per sentence as this one does,
it is the right length however long it is.

What else to notice: proof lives in the **table**, in the report's **own
words**; prose appears only where an element is contested or missing; no section
explains a legal concept; no fact is stated twice.

> **⚠ CASE-CRITICAL — recovered weapon is an airsoft pistol:** the dangerous-weapon
> element is unlikely to survive as charged. *See Charge 1.*

## 1. SUMMARY

### 1A. The incident

At approximately 11:40 p.m. on Tuesday, June 2, 2026, Jane Doe 1 was walking
alone from her apartment to her car, parked in the surface lot in the 400 block
of Sample Street. She told Ofc. Reyes the lot was dark — *"the lot lights were
out"* — and that she noticed two men crossing toward her from the sidewalk
behind her. The taller man came to within a few feet, **displayed what she
described as a black handgun** at waist level, and said *"give it here."* The
second, shorter man stood at her driver's door without speaking. Jane Doe 1
stated she handed over her purse immediately; she was not touched, and she
reported no injury. Before the taking, she heard the shorter man say *"run
that"* to the taller one.

Both men then ran east on foot toward Example Avenue. Jane Doe 1 used her phone
to call 911 from the lot at 11:43 p.m. She described the gunman as a man in his
twenties wearing *"an orange sweater"* and the second man as wearing dark
clothing. The purse contained her wallet, keys, and phone charger; total value
was not itemized in the report.

### 1B. Investigative chronology

- **06/02/2026, 2343** — 911 call received from the 400 block of Sample Street;
  dispatched as armed robbery from person. Ofc. Reyes arrived 2347 and took the
  initial account above (Narr. #1).
- **06/02/2026, 2351** — Ofc. Reyes stopped John Doe 1 and a second subject four
  blocks east on Example Avenue, **eleven minutes after the call**. **The purse
  was recovered from Doe 1's waistband.** An item logged as *"airsoft pistol,
  orange tip removed"* was recovered from the codefendant (Narr. #1; property
  log).
- **06/03/2026, 0012** — **Street show-up** at the stop location. Jane Doe 1
  identified Doe 1 as the gunman. **Doe 1 was booked in a red hoodie**; no
  orange sweater was recovered from either subject or the route (Narr. #1).
- **06/03/2026** — Doe 1 interviewed at intake. He stated only *"I found the
  purse."* He made no statement about the weapon and did not name the
  codefendant (Det. Alvarez supplement).
- **06/04/2026** — **Photo array** shown to John Doe 2, a bystander who called in
  a second 911 report. He *"was unable to identify any of the subjects"* (Det.
  Alvarez supplement).
- **06/04/2026** — Supplement notes the 400-block businesses *"were not
  canvassed"* for surveillance. **No canvass has been recorded since**, and no
  footage is in the file.
- **06/09/2026** — Property log records the airsoft pistol into evidence. **No
  firearms-unit examination has been requested or reported.**

## 2. CHARGE ANALYSIS

### Charge 1 — Robbery with a dangerous weapon, G.S. 14-87 (Class D)
*Elements per N.C.P.I.—Crim. 217.20 (rev. 2023).*

| # | Element (pattern language) | Status | Proof in the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | unlawfully took personal property from the person or presence of another | **SUPPORTED** | *"He took my purse right off my shoulder"* — Jane Doe 1 |
| 2 | without the victim's voluntary consent, by endangering or threatening her life with the use or threatened use of a firearm or other dangerous weapon | **NOT SUPPORTED** | Recovered item logged as *"airsoft pistol, orange tip removed"* — Ofc. Reyes |
| 3 | the taker knew he was not entitled to the property | **SUPPORTED** | Purse recovered from Doe 1's waistband at stop |
| 4 | intending to deprive the owner of its use permanently | **SUPPORTED** | Flight from scene with the property |

**Element 2 — NOT SUPPORTED.** No fact establishes the item's capacity to
produce death or great bodily harm; the report describes only appearance
(*"looked like a real gun"* — Jane Doe 1). A firearms-unit examination of the
airsoft pistol, or evidence it was used as a bludgeon, would be needed. As the
record stands, **common law robbery (N.C.P.I.—Crim. 217.10) is supported on
every element** and is the appropriate charge.

### Charge 2 — Conspiracy to commit robbery, G.S. 14-2.4 (Class E)
*Elements per N.C.P.I.—Crim. 202.80 (rev. 2019).*

| # | Element (pattern language) | Status | Proof in the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | defendant and at least one other person entered into an agreement | **SUPPORTED** | Codefendant to Doe 1 before approach: *"run that"* — Jane Doe 1 |
| 2 | to commit robbery | **SUPPORTED** | Same statement, immediately preceding the taking |
| 3 | the parties intended the agreement be carried out at the time it was made | **SUPPORTED** | Coordinated approach, positioning at driver's door, joint flight |

*Conspiracy survives independent of the weapon problem; the object offense need
not be completed as charged.*

## 3. WEAKNESSES

- **Clothing mismatch** (see 1B, 06/03 show-up). The single strongest
  identification attack available to the defense; expect it on cross.
- **Show-up procedure.** The 06/03 identification was a street show-up, not an
  array — inherently more suggestive, and the only positive ID in the file.
- **Failed array.** John Doe 2's non-identification on 06/04 cuts against the
  one ID the State has, and he is an otherwise neutral witness.
- **Surveillance never canvassed.** Per 1B, 06/04 — recovery may still be
  possible, but retention windows are short and no canvass has been ordered.
- **Weapon characterization.** See Charge 1 — the report calls the item a
  *"handgun"* in the narrative and an *"airsoft pistol"* on the property log.

*Also checked and clean: chain of custody, response time, translation, officer-observed injuries (none reported).*

## 4. DEFENSES

No affirmative defense is supported. Doe 1 made no statement claiming
self-defense, accident, or alibi; the report records only *"I found the purse."*
That is a defense to the taking rather than an affirmative defense and is
undercut by the timing and the codefendant's *"run that."* Mere presence is
unavailable to Doe 1, who was found in possession of the property. Nothing in
the record raises duress or consent.

## 5. IDENTIFICATION

Doe 1 is tied to the offense by the show-up ID, recovery of the purse on his
person, and proximity in time and place. Against that: the show-up procedure,
the clothing mismatch, John Doe 2's non-identification, and night lighting at
the scene (*"the lot lights were out"* — Ofc. Reyes). Identity is supportable
but will be contested; the property recovery, not the eyewitness, is the
stronger link.

## 6. BOTTOM LINE

| Charge | Probable cause | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Robbery w/ dangerous weapon, G.S. 14-87 | **No — element 2 fails** | *Proceed on common law robbery, G.S. 14-87.1* |
| Conspiracy to commit robbery, G.S. 14-2.4 | **Yes** | *Proceed as charged* |

Resolve the weapon characterization before charging — a firearms-unit
examination of the airsoft pistol governs whether 14-87 is viable at all. The
canvass for 400-block surveillance should be completed while it is still
retrievable. No additional offenses outside the charges requested were noted.
